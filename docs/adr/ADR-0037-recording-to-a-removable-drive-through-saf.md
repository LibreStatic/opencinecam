# ADR-0037: Recording to a removable drive through SAF

- Status: Proposed (Delivery 1 of OCC-PLAN-072 is implemented; the device acceptance on the Razr Fold is pending)
- Date: 2026-11-08
- Related requirements: OCC-STORAGE-001
- Related plan: OCC-PLAN-072
- Extends ADR-0013 (MediaStore, SAF and throughput qualification) and ADR-0011 (single-owner muxer and file finalization).

## Context

ADR-0013 writes every take through MediaStore pending rows on `VOLUME_EXTERNAL_PRIMARY` and uses SAF only for destinations the operator picks for one-off work (the storage benchmark, the CinemaDNG export). Long MP4 takes and RAW video fill the phone's storage quickly, and operators want to record to a drive on USB-C, as a cinema camera records to its media. The evidence is in OCC-PLAN-072 §3 (Razr Fold, Android 16, a self-powered exFAT disk).

## Decision

1. **SAF is the only route to a removable drive.** The operator picks one folder with `ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT_TREE`, answered by the system DocumentsUI (`com.google.android.documentsui`, then `com.android.documentsui`). A tree is adopted only if it comes from `com.android.externalstorage.documents` and is not on the phone's own storage. The app keeps the persisted read and write permission. The tree, its volume id and its label live in their own SharedPreferences, never in presets.
2. **exFAT, checked at adoption.** A scratch file is grown past 4 GiB with `ftruncate`, read back and deleted. A drive that refuses or clamps the length is rejected with a message to format it as exFAT.
3. **One folder per take, with final names from the start.** A take is written to `<picked>/OpenCineCam/<stem>/` (video, WAV or FLAC, and JSON sidecars together), and stills to `<picked>/OpenCineCam/Stills/<stem>/`. Every document is created as `application/octet-stream` under its final name, so the provider adds no extension. A hidden `.occ-in-progress` document in the folder stands in for MediaStore's `IS_PENDING`. Deleting it is the commit, both when a take finishes and when recovery publishes it as incomplete. Document URIs therefore never change after creation.
4. **The recovery journals know the destination.** `recording-recovery.sqlite` and `still-recovery.sqlite` move to version 2, with nullable `tree`, `volume` and `folder` columns. Phone rows keep them null and behave as before. Recovery defers a drive row whose volume is not mounted or whose permission was lost, and never treats a drive that does not answer as empty. When a volume is mounted, recovery runs again for it. Recovery journals bound phone and drive entries separately (128 each), so entries deferred on drives that never return cannot refuse a phone take or still; when a new drive entry finds the drive bound full, the oldest entry recovery would defer is forgotten (the journal row only, with a log line; its folder and marker stay on that drive), and the take is refused only if every drive entry could still be resolved. Switching or clearing the destination, or changing the CinemaDNG export folder, keeps the folder permission of any tree an uncommitted journal entry references (or the same folder under the drive's other volume name), so a deferred take stays recoverable; that permission is not released when the entry is later resolved.
5. **The destination is resolved per take.** At take start (and per still) the service resolves the configured destination against the mounted volumes and the held permission. If the drive cannot be used, the take goes to the phone with a reason the HUD shows. Free space, admission and the time left read the destination of the take.
6. **A drive lost mid-take stops the take and keeps it.** A volume `ejecting` event for the take's volume, or a write that fails on the drive (ENODEV, which can come before the event), ends the take through the existing early-stop path with a `recording-drive` failure (`drive-disconnected` or `drive-write-failed`). What reached the drive is recovered as an incomplete take when the drive returns. vold kills any process that still holds a descriptor on a removed volume (SIGINT about 1.3 s after its scan), so every write descriptor on the drive (the take's MP4, WAV or FLAC, and `.ocraw`) is registered by volume and closed as soon as the drive leaves or a write to it fails, before publication or any other cleanup. The volume monitor does it on its own callback thread, not after the main-thread hop; a writer meets the closed descriptor as a write failure, and a RAW take released this way is left journalled for recovery. The same registry holds the long-lived read descriptors on a drive take (a WebDAV upload, a file open on the network drive, a CinemaDNG export, RAW playback) and the short transfers of a still. Because background work can run in a process with neither the capture service nor the gallery, the first registration starts a volume monitor that lives as long as the process (`DriveDescriptorWatch`, given the application context by `OpenCineCamApplication`). The live take's journal group is released (`relinquish`) once its writers are done, so the running process can recover it without a restart. With *If the drive disconnects* set to Continue on the phone (Delivery 4), the take is restarted rather than its muxer switched: once the drive take's stop has fully finished (its finalization, foreground stop and transfer reservation release), the service starts a new take on the phone through the ordinary start path, with the destination forced to the phone even if the drive came back, the stem `<parent stem>_cont1`, the parent's frozen capture names and slate, and the same mode, settings and audio; timecode continues. The gap is about one to two seconds. Its clip sidecar carries an optional `continuation {of, previousName, index, reason}` block naming the drive part (whose sidecar is never rewritten); a RAW continuation records the same link as `continuation*` keys in its `.ocraw` static metadata. VIDEO, LOG and HLG continue unless high-speed; RAW video continues only when its configuration is qualified on the phone (`mediastore-documents`, not risky) and the phone has room; time-lapse and high-speed stop and save, and the end message says why. Every trigger (the volume event, the sidecar writer failure, ENODEV at the stop, the RAW writer error) converges on the one stop of the take, which arms a single continuation token at most once; an operator stop, a record press in the gap, a refused admission, backgrounding or the service stopping drop it, and a token not consumed within 15 s is treated as gone.
7. **RAW video records to the drive once the drive is qualified** (OCC-PLAN-072 Delivery 3). A RAW take resolves its destination as an MP4 take does. On a usable drive it writes `<tree>/OpenCineCam/<stem>/<stem>.ocraw` (created `application/octet-stream` under its final name, opened "rw" and wrapped in a `DuplexFileChannel`, never renamed) beside the folder's `.occ-in-progress` marker, and is gated by the drive's own qualification records, `destinationId = "saf:<VOLUME>"` (the volume id upper-cased). With a drive configured but absent or without its grant, the take falls back to the phone with the usual notice and is gated by the phone's records (`mediastore-documents`, untouched, so phone-only operators see no change); a configuration not qualified there is refused. Every RAW lookup (gate, RES/FPS chooser, Automatic, settings row, qualification prompt) uses the destination the next RAW take would use and refreshes when the drive mounts or leaves; a usable drive with no records makes RAW video a candidate whose prompt qualifies the drive. A drive qualification is a full run: camera checks are kept in memory per run, so they are not reused from the phone's qualification. Its scratch files go in the hidden `<tree>/OpenCineCam/.occ-qualify/` folder, which is purged at the start of every qualification and when the drive mounts; nothing else is touched. The storage rest and heat heuristics are unchanged, though they were tuned on the phones' UFS and not on USB drives. A drive RAW take is journalled in `recording-recovery.sqlite` (schema v2 unchanged; recovery tells it from an MP4 take by its `.ocraw`) and registered in `RawVideoRecovery`'s live set. Publish deletes the marker; a take with no frame removes its folder. When the drive is mounted again (or before the next RAW take), recovery repairs the file with `recoverRawTakeFile`, then publishes it if it holds a frame and removes it otherwise. It defers while the volume is absent or the grant lost, and a drive that does not answer is never treated as empty. A write failure on the drive stops the take like a volume event (Stop and save). The RAW repair scan runs outside the recording journal's lock: the entry is marked as being repaired, so takes start and stop meanwhile and a concurrent pass skips it.
8. **Takes on the drive are enrolled in the WebDAV outbox like the phone's** (Delivery 2). The outbox persists the canonical document URI (a tree-based `com.android.externalstorage.documents` URI under the adopted tree, with no `.` or `..` segment and no alias encoding). The finalized signal is the folder's marker: a drive artifact is pending while `.occ-in-progress` is in its folder and finalized once the group commit has deleted it, and its size and descriptor checks are those of a MediaStore item, on the descriptor actually opened. A document gone from a drive that answers is a missing source, as on the phone. A drive that does not answer is transient: the artifact keeps its state (QUEUED, or UNCERTAIN after a remote check), the transfer reads "Waiting for the USB drive" and the automatic upload retries with its usual backoff and attempt cap. It never becomes SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE, which waits for the user, and since each bundle is its own unique work it never holds back the phone's takes. The same rules let the gallery share a drive take (re-read from its folder), make its proxy on the phone (the original is never touched; an unplugged drive fails the job with "USB drive not connected") and offer its RAW takes on the network drive.
9. **A candidate for USB drives while one is the destination.** When a drive is plugged in, Android asks which app should handle it if any installed app (a file manager, say) declares `USB_DEVICE_ATTACHED`, and that chooser covers the app, mid-shoot included. No app can hide another app's chooser, so the manifest declares `.UsbDriveAttachAlias`, an exported activity with no display (no launcher entry) with `USB_DEVICE_ATTACHED` and a USB mass storage filter (`@xml/usb_drive_filter`, class 8). The operator answers OpenCineCam with Always once; the system then brings the app to the front on attach. The activity finishes at once, leaving whatever was in front as it was, and opens `MainActivity` first only when the task is empty; it is not an alias of `MainActivity`, because the system starts the handler on every attach and an alias stacked a new `MainActivity` over the settings or the folder picker each time (seen on the Razr with a hub that re-enumerates). It is disabled in the manifest and enabled only while a USB folder is the configured destination (`RecordingDestinationStore.syncDriveAttachHandler`, after an adoption, a clear and once per process at start), because as the only candidate the app would make Android show a confirmation on every attach to operators who never record to a drive. Android keeps the Always choice per USB device, so each new drive asks once.

## Rejected alternatives

- **MediaStore on the USB volume.** It worked on the Razr because the volume was `VISIBLE_FOR_WRITE`, but AOSP does not promise that for USB drives, and an OEM or a later release can withdraw it.
- **Raw paths under `/storage/<UUID>` or `/mnt/media_rw`.** They need `MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` or are unreachable to apps, against ADR-0027.
- **`.part` files renamed at publish.** `DocumentsContract.renameDocument` changes the document id, so every URI already recorded in prepared artifacts and sidecars would break. The provider also appends an extension from the MIME type (`x.mp4.part` as `video/mp4` became `x.mp4.part.mp4`).
- **Treating a missing volume as an empty one in recovery.** Today's pass failed with "Recording volume unavailable"; deleting or publishing rows for an absent drive would lose the take or the evidence.

## Consequences

- Positive: an operator can record MP4, audio sidecars, stills and qualified RAW video to a drive, and a take pulled mid-way keeps what reached the drive and, if chosen, goes on on the phone in a linked `_cont1` take.
- Negative: each drive needs its own RAW qualification, a full run of about the phone's length, and a slow drive (a ~100 MB/s hard disk) qualifies only small crops.
- Negative: two storage backends (MediaStore and SAF) sit behind the recovery member API, and the journals carry destination columns. A crash between creating a take folder and journalling it can leave an empty folder on the drive.
- Operational: drive behaviour depends on the cable, the power and the drive. A plain OTG adapter dropped a self-powered disk every few seconds on the Razr; a powered hub fixed it. Device acceptance for the drive is listed in `docs/device-validation.md`.

## Revisit conditions

- MediaStore guarantees write access to USB volumes on every supported release.
- A device shows `MediaMuxer` blocking instead of failing on a removed drive.
- Device runs show the continuation gap well above two seconds, or a camera that drops the session on restart: then a muxer or channel switch on the running session is worth its complexity.
- Drive qualifications show the heat heuristics misjudging USB drives (they were tuned on UFS). The storage rest rule already did: on the Razr a rested WD Elements hard disk swept at a 91.7 MB/s median with a 42.0 MB/s first percentile, which the rule read as a write cache still flushing and ended the run with "Storage needs to rest". A drive's run now ignores that rule and goes on to the long runs. A configuration whose stream the sweep's median cannot carry with rule v2's margin gets no long run: the sweep decides it (risky when its camera check passed), and the long runs judge only the configurations the sweep allows against what the drive sustains.
